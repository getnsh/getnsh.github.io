One deliberate calorie deficit. Five lifts a week to keep the muscle you already have. A seven-day trend that never flinches at a single weigh-in.

It is not a tracker you fill in. It is a protocol that knows what time it is, what today is, and what you have actually done — and tells you what a tap means before it writes it.

01 — what it is

Your whole day, resolved

Five screens on one device. No feed, no settings maze, nothing buried. The day's twenty-one steps sit in a single ledger that reads itself out — what is done, what is now, what is waiting on something else, and what does not apply today at all.

today
The session, the week, and the day. One thing is offered at a time.
the ledger
Every step, with its state printed on the row. Nothing to learn before acting.
consequence
Out of place? It says what the tap means, then lets you do it anyway.
02 — the week

A drum, not a list

Three weeks of runway either side of today, so what is coming is as visible as what is due. The day at the centre stands up and the rest fall away around the drum. Drag it, or use the arrow keys.

Tapping a day opens what it holds — every movement, its demo and the load it will ask for — so tomorrow is never a surprise at 04:25.

03 — the part that makes it different

The day adapts. A tap has a meaning.

Most checklists are twenty-one independent booleans. Tick "lights out" at two in the afternoon and nothing objects, nothing records when, and nothing downstream changes. That is a to-do list wearing a protocol's clothes.

DEFICIT resolves the whole day from three things: which steps are elastic — they ride on the wake that actually happened — and which are fixed, because the gym opens when it opens; what each step stands on; and what time it is now.

05 — when things go wrong

Six rules, and none of them scold you

The protocol assumes life happens. Every rule is stated up front, visible in the app, and fires from logged evidence rather than a guess.

R1
if you slept under six hours
Train anyway at 70% load, nothing near failure. The session counts; the promotion chip goes dark.
R2
if you missed a session entirely
Resume the schedule. Never double up.
R3
if two consecutive misses, or fatigue signs for a week
Take the deload week early — offered, never imposed.
R4
if the trend is flat for three weeks
Audit the logging first — untracked bites are the usual culprit — then recalculate for the lighter body, then add 1,000 steps, and only then trim 150 kcal.
R5
if joint pain persists into the next day
Stop-and-substitute. Swap the movement from a 32-item library, keep the session. Every swap is counted, because routing around a niggle forever is how it becomes an injury.
R6
if falling asleep takes 30+ minutes on caffeine days
Pull the caffeine cutoff from 11:30 back to 09:00.
06 — how to use it

A day, start to finish

The short version: weigh in every morning, answer the four-tap check-in, log your sets, take the walk, and tell it when you eat. That is the whole job. Everything else it works out.

on waking
Tick wake. If you are late, the elastic morning rebases around you and the app names what the late start has already run past — the fixed times do not move.
before anything else
Four taps: how you slept, fatigue, soreness, motivation. This is what lets rules 1, 3 and 6 fire at all. Then the scale — it never gates the session.
the session
Sets are written the moment you tap them, not at the end, so a killed app loses nothing. Log reps and what you had left. A promotion arms itself and waits for the next session rather than committing into this one.
through the day
Answer feedings as they happen — ate or skipped, both are answers. Take the walk; the plan rates it above the lifting session.
evening
Wind down, and lights out follows when you actually wound down rather than a time written months ago. Log it late and it tells you how long that leaves before your alarm.

Missed a whole day? Say so, or say nothing. An unlogged past day stays unknown — the app never files a confession on your behalf. There are no streaks to break. The only number that matters is a total that cannot go down.

07 — safe by construction

It cannot send your data anywhere

This is not a privacy policy, it is an architecture. There is no networking code in the app. On Android it does not hold the INTERNET permission at all — the operating system would refuse the connection even if it tried. The three permissions it does ask for are these, and nothing else:

notificationsthe day's cues — capped at four, only from high-stakes steps
vibratehaptics on a logged set
run at bootso the day's reminders survive a restart
  • No account, no sign-in, no email address.
  • No server. There is nowhere for the data to go.
  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no third-party SDKs.
  • Nothing syncs between devices — your log lives on the one phone.

The trade-off is real and worth stating plainly: because there is no server, there is no backup. Lose or wipe the phone and the logbook goes with it. The single reset in Settings deletes everything, and nothing survives it.

08 — getting it

Installing

Android

A single APK — no store, no account. Open the file and allow the install when your phone asks. Android 8 and up, every CPU type included, so essentially any phone from 2017 onward.

download the apk · 21 mb
all releases

Your phone will warn you about installing from an unknown source, and Play Protect may add an "unrecognised developer" prompt. Both are expected for an app that did not come from Google, and both have a way through. Neither means anything is wrong.

iOS

Built and installed from source with Xcode. Apple does not allow APK-style sideloading, so an iPhone needs the project, a Mac and a free Apple ID.

On a free developer account the app stops launching after seven days and has to be reinstalled. A paid account extends that to a year. Apple's limit, not the app's.

09 — being straight with you

What this is not

  • Not medical advice. It prescribes a calorie deficit and a lifting programme. If you are pregnant, have a heart condition, diabetes, a history of disordered eating, or take medication that interacts with fasting or exercise, talk to a doctor before running it.
  • Not a calorie database. It counts feedings and protein against a target; it does not identify your food for you.
  • Not a store app. A personal project, self-signed, given away as a file. There is no support desk.
  • Not a coach that will chase you. It has an opinion and states it once. Ignoring it costs nothing, and it will not turn your day into a scoreboard.

One principle behind all of it: no rule in the engine can reach a single day's weight. That is enforced by the build, not by good intentions — the logic lives in a module that physically cannot see the database.
